Private credit has grown from roughly $300 billion in 2010 to around $1.7 trillion today, according to Preqin. Post-2008 bank regulation, especially Basel III capital requirements, made it more expensive for banks to hold leveraged loans, so mid-market borrowers turned to non-bank lenders. At the same time, a decade of near-zero rates pushed pension funds and insurers toward higher-yielding private assets. Floating-rate structures also made the asset class attractive when rates began rising in 2022. The result is a market that now rivals high-yield bonds in size.
